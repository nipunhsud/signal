# The breakout bar, the staircase, and the length floor

September 2026. Four questions, one pool, one method.

RSKD closed at $7.01 on 22 September, up 10.4% on the day and 5.2M shares
against a 1.7M average. That close sat 0.4% above the $6.98 pivot of a
2.2-week, 17%-deep grade-A base — a base that was itself the third step
stacked on two earlier ones, the first of which had already been emailed on
3 September and was down 6.4%. Four things about that row looked worth
measuring rather than arguing about.

## Method

The same pool and arithmetic as the depth-cut and institutional-activity
studies, so the numbers sit beside those. Every base the detector resolves,
1985 to date, 5,485 symbols, keeping only the graded shape: blue-sky pivot,
depth at or under 25%, close above a rising 200-day, 100k average volume.
Entry is the breakout close. Win is a positive 20-bar return. A fail is a 7%
touch inside 20 bars. Profit factor is gross gains over gross losses with each
loss floored at −7%. Reach is a close 20% above entry inside 60 bars.

Baseline for the 25-bar-plus pool: 44,434 breakouts, 55.9% positive, 27.8%
touched the fail level, 13.9% reached +20%, profit factor 1.84.

## 1. A big day into the pivot is not a penalty. It is a variance multiplier.

| The bar's own gain | n | positive | touched fail | reached +20% | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2% | 22,340 | 57.3% | 20.8% | 8.7% | 1.72 |
| 2 to 4% | 12,757 | 55.1% | 30.5% | 15.8% | 1.83 |
| 4 to 6% | 4,413 | 54.5% | 38.7% | 22.4% | 2.00 |
| 6 to 10% | 2,869 | 53.3% | 40.6% | 24.8% | 2.12 |
| 10%+ | 1,600 | 51.7% | 46.8% | 24.5% | 2.03 |

The hit rate falls steadily and the fail rate more than doubles, yet the profit
factor rises. A big bar buys a wider distribution, not a worse one. Nothing
here argues for refusing the row.

## 2. Clearance is the thing the big bar did not buy.

| Close past the pivot | n | positive | touched fail | reached +20% | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 0.5% | 16,389 | 56.1% | 23.1% | 9.7% | 1.67 |
| 0.5 to 1% | 9,436 | 56.7% | 25.0% | 11.6% | 1.80 |
| 1 to 2% | 8,700 | 56.5% | 29.1% | 15.5% | 1.95 |
| 2 to 4% | 4,944 | 54.5% | 35.9% | 20.5% | 1.97 |
| 4 to 8% | 1,867 | 52.3% | 41.9% | 25.0% | 1.95 |
| 8%+ | 595 | 49.4% | 46.6% | 27.9% | 2.19 |

The same finding the deep band produced: a decisive clearance is worth more
than a marginal one, in the opposite direction to the "extended" instinct. The
weakest slice on this table is the one that barely got over the line.

RSKD spent a 10.4% day arriving at its pivot and closed 0.4% past it, which
puts it in the strongest bucket of the first table and the weakest of the
second. That pairing is measurable on its own: 803 cases of a 6%-plus bar
closing under 1% past the pivot ran 53.4% positive, 44.1% fail touches, 23.8%
reach, profit factor 1.96.

## 3. The staircase does not matter. The first cut that said it did was wrong.

Comparing each breakout against the previous one in the same name, using the
previous one's 60-bar return, produced a spectacular split: profit factor 3.20
when the earlier step had worked, 0.10 when it had not. That number is
worthless. The earlier step's 60-bar window overlaps the later breakout, so it
already contains the answer.

Re-cut using only what was knowable on the morning of the new breakout — is
price still above the previous breakout's close, and did that one ever touch
its own fail level:

| | n | positive | touched fail | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 46,134 | 55.8% | 27.7% | 1.84 |
| a step within 120 bars | 23,140 | 56.0% | 26.0% | 1.79 |
| still above the prior step | 23,012 | 56.0% | 25.9% | 1.79 |
| back under the prior step | 128 | 47.7% | 43.8% | 1.27 |
| prior step never failed | 13,867 | 56.5% | 23.1% | 1.75 |
| prior step touched its fail | 9,273 | 55.1% | 30.3% | 1.83 |

Nothing. A base stacked on a recent breakout is an ordinary base, and a base
stacked on one that already failed is not worse than one stacked on a winner.
Steps close together, within 40 bars, were mildly better than the pool at 2.17.

## 4. There is no length floor to find.

Grade A carries no minimum base length, and every number published behind it
was measured on bases of 25 bars or more. Dropping the filter and re-running:

| Base length | n | positive | touched fail | reached +20% | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| under 15 bars | 24,930 | 53.7% | 33.9% | 17.3% | 1.80 |
| 15 to 20 | 14,794 | 54.7% | 33.3% | 18.4% | 1.96 |
| 20 to 25 | 9,537 | 55.0% | 32.1% | 16.9% | 1.88 |
| 25 to 35 | 12,417 | 55.5% | 31.3% | 16.6% | 1.91 |
| 35 to 50 | 10,611 | 55.5% | 29.9% | 16.0% | 1.90 |
| 50 to 80 | 9,729 | 54.8% | 28.7% | 13.0% | 1.72 |
| 80+ | 11,677 | 57.5% | 21.4% | 9.7% | 1.79 |

Short bases fail more often and pay more when they work. Raising a floor from
10 bars to 25 moves the profit factor from 1.85 to 1.84 and throws away half
the sample. There is no floor worth adding.

What short bases do change is the shape of the distribution, and RSKD sits at
the far end of it:

| | n | positive | touched fail | reached +20% | PF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| under 25 bars, bar 6%+ | 5,043 | 52.1% | 53.9% | 35.8% | 2.52 |
| under 25 bars, bar 6%+, clear under 1% | 785 | 49.3% | 56.1% | 35.7% | 2.45 |
| under 25 bars, 14%+ deep | 4,819 | 50.7% | 63.7% | 42.1% | 2.70 |
| under 25 bars, volume 2x+ | 8,360 | 54.1% | 39.6% | 24.3% | 2.20 |

A short, deep base breaking out on a large bar is close to a coin flip on the
20-bar close, touches its fail level more often than not, and reaches +20% three
times as often as the pool. Profit factor 2.45 against a 1.85 baseline.

## What this changes

Not the gate. The measured answer to "should the screen refuse a row that ran
10% into its pivot, on a 2.2-week third step" is no, on every cut.

What it changes is what the screen says about such a row. A grade-A card
quotes the pooled 54.8%, and for this row the conditional numbers are a 49%
hold rate, a 56% fail-level touch and a 36% chance of +20%. The average is
the wrong sentence for a row this far from the average. The dossier already
carries per-factor evidence; the headline should follow the row's own shape
rather than its grade.

## What did not work

Testing whether the base actually consolidated or merely traced a V — deepest
pullback inside the recovery leg, bars closing in the lowest third, where the
low was made, recovery-leg volume. The V slice held up better than the pool
(58.7% positive, 14.7% fail touches) and ran far less (5.0% reach, profit
factor 1.65), but the slice is almost entirely shallow bases; in the deeper
half it has 102 cases. No usable separator, recorded so it is not re-run.
